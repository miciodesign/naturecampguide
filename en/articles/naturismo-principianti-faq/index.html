Naturism for beginners: the most common questions answered

"What if I feel embarrassed?" "Can I come alone?" "What happens if it rains?" The questions everyone asks before their first time — with honest answers.

Before a first visit to a naturist campsite, everyone has the same questions. Some are practical, others are fears that seem large but dissolve quickly. Here are the most honest answers we can give.

What if I feel embarrassed?

It happens to almost everyone in the very first minutes. Then it passes. The fact that everyone is in the same situation speeds the process enormously: there is no "clothed" norm against which to feel different. Most people report that after 20–30 minutes they stop thinking about it.

Can I come alone?

Yes, and it is more common than you might think. Many naturists visit campsites alone, especially the more experienced ones. Naturist campsites are social, welcoming environments — striking up a conversation is often easier than at a conventional campsite, because the informal setting lowers barriers.

What if I have an unwanted physical reaction?

This is the question many people have but nobody wants to ask. The practical answer: lie face-down on your towel, dive into the water, or wait for it to pass. It happens rarely after the very first minutes because the context is not erotic. Naturist campsites are not sexualised environments.

Do I have to undress straight away?

No. There is no rule requiring you to strip at the gate. You can undress when you are ready — on your pitch, at the beach, wherever. Many people arrive clothed, set up camp and undress once they are already comfortable.

What happens if it rains?

As at any campsite: you put something on. At naturist campsites there is no rule requiring nudity in bad weather. Many have covered facilities, bars and common rooms where you can spend rainy days.

Can I bring a swimsuit?

At most naturist campsites the main areas (pool, beach, common zones) are nude-only. Some campsites have "mixed" areas or allow swimwear in the restaurant area. Check the specific rules before you go. In any case, packing a swimsuit is always sensible for unexpected situations.

Is it safe for women travelling alone?

Naturist campsites are generally considered very safe for women. Rules are clear, the community is attentive, and any harassing behaviour is taken seriously. The code of conduct — no staring, no unwanted advances — is enforced more strictly than in many other settings. This does not mean unpleasant situations never occur, but the culture of naturist campsites is structurally more respectful than many other summer environments.

What if my body is not "perfect"?

Naturism is perhaps the only environment where body variety is truly normal and unremarked upon. You will see bodies of all ages, shapes and sizes — and that is exactly the point. Nobody judges. The experience is often liberating for those who arrive with body insecurities.

Can I take photos?

No, not without explicit consent. This rule is strictly enforced at all naturist campsites. For landscape photos or selfies, always check that no other people are in frame. When in doubt, ask.

How do I know if a campsite is a "real" naturist one?

INF- or ENF-affiliated campsites are the officially recognised ones. All campsites in our guide are verified. Be wary of sites that call themselves "naturist" without affiliation: their expectations and atmosphere can be very different from a traditional naturist campsite.
