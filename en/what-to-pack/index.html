What to pack for a naturist campsite

Less than you think. Naturism simplifies the suitcase, but some things are compulsory and others make the difference between a comfortable holiday and an awkward one. Here is the list we would use, by situation.

Compulsory, or nearly

  • Sitting towel

    Rule number one of naturism: always sit on your own towel, at the bar and by the pool too. Bring two, one small enough for your bag.

    See on Amazon

  • INF naturist card

    Required at many Italian and German campsites, recommended everywhere. Issued by your national federation (British Naturism, FFN, DFK) and valid for a year.

  • SPF 50 sunscreen

    Without a swimsuit, parts that have never seen the sun will burn. Get a large bottle and reapply every two hours.

    See on Amazon

  • Washable sandals or flip-flops

    For showers, sanitary blocks and hot paths. Choose a quick-drying model.

    See on Amazon

Beach and sea

  • Rock shoes

    Istria, Calabria, Liguria: rocky and pebbly coasts. The beaches at Solaris, Pizzo Greco and Guvano all need them.

    See on Amazon

  • Parasol with screw anchor

    Many naturist beaches have no shade and no hire; a screw anchor holds in Atlantic and North Sea winds.

    See on Amazon

  • Waterproof beach backpack

    For the 20 minutes of sand at Maspalomas or the path at Montalivet: hands free and phone dry.

    See on Amazon

  • Insulated bottle

    No kiosk in the central stretch of many beaches: a litre of cold water each.

    See on Amazon

Campsite

  • Portable fridge

    On a pitch with electricity included, an electric cool box beats any cool bag.

    See on Amazon

  • CEE camping extension lead

    European hook-ups use the blue 16 A CEE plug; you need 25 metres and an adapter for national sockets.

    See on Amazon

  • Head torch

    Many naturist campsites are poorly lit at night, La Sablière for one.

    See on Amazon

  • Washing line and pegs

    For the swimsuits you won't use and the towels you always will.

    See on Amazon

Small things people forget

  • Sarong or light kimono

    For moments when covering up is required: restaurant, shop, textile zones of mixed resorts such as Koversada.

    See on Amazon

  • Mosquito repellent

    Pine forests and rivers, from Euronat to the Cèze, are full of them at dusk.

    See on Amazon

  • €1 coins

    For token showers (Hilsbachtal) and washing machines.

  • Paper guide

    Where Wi-Fi is paid or absent: the INF-FNI naturist guide or a campsite guide.
